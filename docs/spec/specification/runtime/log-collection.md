## Purpose and observable model

A session runs across more than one thread, and each thread keeps its own logs
([execution.md](./execution.md)). The thread that hits an error is usually not the one holding the logs
that explain it. Running with less isolation means fewer threads, not fewer sets of logs.

A collection is a best-effort upload trigger across connected runtime roots. Each logger uploads its own shared store. An optional coordination service asks its other stores and connected roots to upload too. Independent top-level roots are separate unless explicitly connected. Standalone loggers can upload without a service. Two cases wait for the upload, each for a bounded time: a runtime that aborts its channel participation flushes the logs of every thread it can reach before it disposes ([`REQ-LOG-11-F5TJQC`](log-collection.md#req-log-11-f5tjqc)), and a disposing runtime flushes its logs before its connection closes ([`REQ-RUNTIME-3-VQXW59` (Lifecycle convergence)](execution.md#req-runtime-3-vqxw59)).

## Requirements and invariants

**<a id="inv-log-1-p4wt6r"></a>`INV-LOG-1-P4WT6R` — A collection reaches connected roots.** An accepted upload trigger MUST schedule the attached stores and propagate through live root connections, including inline roots. Roots MUST suppress already handled generations. Propagation is best effort: it does not promise that every reachable root has uploaded before the caller returns. Only the abort flush of [`REQ-LOG-11-F5TJQC`](log-collection.md#req-log-11-f5tjqc) and the disposal flush of [`REQ-RUNTIME-3-VQXW59` (Lifecycle convergence)](execution.md#req-runtime-3-vqxw59) wait for that, each only up to its bound.

**<a id="inv-log-2-c7kz9m"></a>`INV-LOG-2-C7KZ9M` — Collections never wait on one another.** Remote gossip triggering MUST NOT wait for acknowledgments. A greater incoming generation is adopted before forwarding, unchanged. Equal or older generations MUST be ignored even after the coalescing window. A fresh local trigger outside that window advances the generation; nearby local triggers share it. Gossip cycles MUST terminate without per-origin request tracking. The abort flush of [`REQ-LOG-11-F5TJQC`](log-collection.md#req-log-11-f5tjqc) is not gossip: it waits for answers and ends cycles by its own flush ID.

**<a id="req-log-1-h2vq8x"></a>`REQ-LOG-1-H2VQ8X` — Logging cleanup preserves surviving owners.** Disposing one logger MUST preserve surviving loggers sharing its resources and reconnect its children to its parent. The last logger detaches the shared store and releases its uploader without clearing buffered entries. Disposing a coordination service detaches its stores and cancels pending coordination; surviving loggers remain usable without it. An explicitly awaited local upload may finish without waiting for other roots. Logging through a disposed logger writes to its realm's console, stores only while a logger of its family lives, never uploads and never fails; a child of a disposed logger is itself disposed. A logger family whose runtime is disposing writes and stores its warnings and errors at info level and starts no upload for them; a live runtime's keep their level. Repeated disposal remains harmless. Root disposal MUST cascade through its root logger’s descendants automatically. Application-instance disposal MUST cascade through its application logger’s descendants. Disposing a logger, its last one included, MUST NOT cancel an upload still running: a realm whose thread lives on keeps its uploads running after disposal. A worker thread that ends MUST cancel every upload of its realm still running, whoever started it, before its loop drains, so no upload holds the thread past its end. A canceled upload MUST end at once, without a retry, and report that it uploaded nothing. Store retention MUST remain bounded by a finite configured pruning limit.

**<a id="req-log-2-n6bj3d"></a>`REQ-LOG-2-N6BJ3D` — The caller receives its local upload outcome.** An awaited logger upload MUST report whether its own attempt succeeded and how many entries it uploaded. It MUST NOT report remote success, failure counts or timeouts. A failed local attempt retains unacknowledged entries. The gossip generation records scheduled work, not successful delivery.

**<a id="req-log-3-t9fm2k"></a>`REQ-LOG-3-T9FM2K` — Writing a log line does not disturb the session.**
Writing an ordinary log line MUST NOT touch the network or disk, block protocol work, or change protocol timing
beyond a little local work. Each thread keeps only so much and throws away the oldest first. An error or explicit report may schedule asynchronous uploading. A store with
nothing new to send MUST perform no HTTP upload or jitter wait.

**<a id="req-log-4-w5xr7q"></a>`REQ-LOG-4-W5XR7Q` — Every line says where it came from.** Every log line
MUST say which session and which participant it belongs to and which thread wrote it, and MUST carry a
timestamp that can be compared against lines from the other threads. If the session or the participant is
only known later, the lines written before that MUST still end up filed under it.

**<a id="req-log-5-st6s0g"></a>`REQ-LOG-5-ST6S0G` — Sending twice does not store twice.** Sending may
happen more than once: if a send is not confirmed it MUST be sent again, and MUST NOT be treated as
stored. Sending lines that are already stored MUST NOT store them twice or put them out of order, and the
receiver MUST NOT have to remember anything between one send and the next.

**<a id="req-log-6-q8ky4n"></a>`REQ-LOG-6-Q8KY4N` — One run's logs never overwrite another's.** Logs from
different sessions, participants, threads, and runs MUST stay apart. A later run MUST NOT overwrite an
earlier one, mix into it, or push it out.

**<a id="req-log-7-m2rc5w"></a>`REQ-LOG-7-M2RC5W` — Nothing unpacks without a limit.** Neither sending nor
reading logs may require holding an unlimited amount unpacked from a limited amount sent. A receiver MUST
stop at its limit while it unpacks, rather than unpack everything and check afterwards, and MUST say when
it left something out rather than quietly return a short log.

**<a id="req-log-9-v6smac"></a>`REQ-LOG-9-V6SMAC` — An application report records its reason.** Explicit report uploading MUST record the application reason in its local logs. It MUST NOT write an aggregate reachability record without remote evidence. Remote delivery is observed at the receiver, independently of the local promise.

**<a id="req-log-10-69ctn1"></a>`REQ-LOG-10-69CTN1` — A thread that is ending waits only for its own logs.** A thread ending because it hit an error MUST ask the threads it can reach to send theirs before it ends, and MUST wait for its own send to finish. It MUST NOT hold its shutdown waiting for any other thread's send. A worker thread that ends for any other reason (its runtime disposes, for example right after an error) MUST first let its own sends still running finish, for at most the flush bound, and a send that is part of a flush (an abort's or a disposal's), or that was asked for while that flush's send still ran, never past that flush's deadline; a bounded flush also bounds a send queued earlier without a deadline, and it ends the jitter wait of a send already running, so an error's send started just before a disposal still leaves ([`REQ-LOG-11-F5TJQC`](log-collection.md#req-log-11-f5tjqc)); it then cancels what is left: otherwise the send an error started during disposal is cancelled in its jitter wait. Its own sends are every send started in the thread, also through a second copy of the SDK modules that a runtime extension built separately from the SDK brings into it. An owner that stops such a thread from outside MUST first wait for it to report that it is closing, at most the flush bound plus a fixed close margin ([`REQ-RUNTIME-7-A2VAR0` (Bounded isolated-context teardown)](execution.md#req-runtime-7-a2var0)). A runtime that aborts its channel participation is not such a thread: it follows [`REQ-LOG-11-F5TJQC`](log-collection.md#req-log-11-f5tjqc).

**<a id="req-log-11-f5tjqc"></a>`REQ-LOG-11-F5TJQC` — An aborting runtime uploads its logs before it disposes.** A runtime aborts its channel participation when a dispute slashes or removes it, when its sync fails and cannot recover, or when it loses a join race. It MUST then ask every thread it can reach to upload its logs now, without the coalescing window, and start this right when it logs the abort, before the application's abort hook runs and before anything is disposed, so the request reaches every thread that exists at the abort. The runtime then disposes as in any other abort: its disposal first stops the producers, then waits for the flush that is already running, and ends the threads only after the flush. The flush has one deadline, the abort time plus a configured bound, and every thread in the flush gets the same deadline. Each thread MUST stop waiting at that deadline, so no thread still answers the flush when disposal ends the threads. The flush itself cancels nothing: an upload still running at the deadline is canceled only when its worker thread ends, and keeps running in a thread that lives on ([`REQ-LOG-1-H2VQ8X`](log-collection.md#req-log-1-h2vq8x)). A thread that is busy and cannot answer holds the flush only until the deadline. The disposal MUST NOT end any thread before every thread has finished its upload or the deadline has passed. Disposal ends the threads, and with them the logs of why the runtime aborted. A neighbour whose connection closes during the flush MUST be skipped, not waited for. A thread's own upload failure is a network outcome that its uploader reports itself, as for any upload. A neighbour's flush failure that is not a closure MUST be reported by the thread that sees it, through its runtime error channel, at the moment it happens: not after the deadline, and not as a failure of the flush. A flush answer that reaches a requester whose connection already closed MUST NOT be reported as a failure. A failure MUST NOT cut the other uploads short: the flush keeps waiting for them until they finish or the deadline passes. The flush itself never fails. Each flush carries its own ID; a thread that is asked again for a flush ID it already handles MUST answer at once, so a cycle ends. A logger without a coordination service MUST refuse to flush, rather than upload only its own store. With log collection off, the abort does not flush and its disposal waits for no thread: every thread runs with its creator's configuration ([`REQ-CONFIG-4-WW7BRX` (Isolated contexts start with their creator's configuration)](configuration.md#req-config-4-ww7brx)), so no thread could upload. Ordinary gossip ([`INV-LOG-2-C7KZ9M`](log-collection.md#inv-log-2-c7kz9m)) does not change.

**<a id="req-log-8-b7vn3j"></a>`REQ-LOG-8-B7VN3J` — Works wherever the runtime works.** Collection MUST
work the same whether the session runs in one thread or several, and on every supported host
([`REQ-RUNTIME-5-WJ1XKK` (Required host environments: browser and Node)](execution.md#req-runtime-5-wj1xkk)) — the same way execution itself has to match
([`INV-RUNTIME-1-AKRHAK` (Execution equivalence)](execution.md#inv-runtime-1-akrhak)). Fewer threads MAY mean fewer sets of logs;
it MUST NOT mean missing the ones that exist.

## Assumptions and constraints

- Gossip usually arrives much faster than the upload coalescing window. This is an accepted best-effort assumption, not a delivery deadline.
- Each service retains one safe integer generation. Newly connected roots synchronize that value. Invalid generations are rejected and exhausted counters must not wrap.
- Higher generations within an active window can share scheduled work. At most one local upload and one follow-up are active; entries beyond the last snapshot can wait for a later generation.

- A thread can crash, hang, or be killed with no warning, including while a collection is running.
- Every thread logs, stores, and uploads with the configuration of the thread that created it
  ([`REQ-CONFIG-4-WW7BRX` (Isolated contexts start with their creator's configuration)](configuration.md#req-config-4-ww7brx)), so a split session collects under one configuration.
- The thread that hits an error often has none of the logs that explain it.
- The abort flush bound is a configured time. A slow or absent receiver can make an abort end up to
  that bound later. An upload still running when the bound passes is canceled when its worker thread
  ends, and its logs are lost ([`REQ-LOG-11-F5TJQC`](log-collection.md#req-log-11-f5tjqc)).
- A host that stops a worker thread from outside waits for its closing report only up to the flush bound
  plus the close margin. A thread whose own sends run past that bound is stopped with them, and those logs
  are lost ([`REQ-LOG-10-69CTN1`](log-collection.md#req-log-10-69ctn1)).
- Logs are there for development and support. They are not protocol state, and no protocol decision may
  depend on them.
- The receiver may be offline, and a send can fail for one thread and work for another.
- Lines get written before the session's identity is known, so
  [`REQ-LOG-4-W5XR7Q`](log-collection.md#req-log-4-w5xr7q) cannot assume it is known when a line is
  written.
- Each thread keeps only so much ([`REQ-LOG-3-T9FM2K`](log-collection.md#req-log-3-t9fm2k)), so a stored
  log can be missing its oldest lines. Where that happened has to be visible, not silent.

## Security considerations

Logs are built out of live protocol data and out of errors, so they are a way for secrets to leak. A log
line MUST NOT carry keys, tokens, or anything else used to authorize a request, copied by accident out of
an error or out of the request that failed. When fields are copied out of an error, they MUST be picked
from a fixed list, never by copying whatever the error happens to expose.

Some of what ends up in a log is chosen by other peers, because a peer affects what a session logs about
it. A receiver MUST NOT trust a field it is sent when deciding where to put the data, MUST limit how much
one send can store, and MUST NOT let one bad send stop the rest of the session's logs from being stored.
A receiver that expects a token MUST refuse a send without one instead of storing it.

A receiver that never answers can hold an aborting runtime only for the abort flush bound
([`REQ-LOG-11-F5TJQC`](log-collection.md#req-log-11-f5tjqc)). The producers stop while the flush waits,
so the wait delays only disposal, not protocol work.

An application instance owns its logger. Setup given a caller logger creates an application child, preserving the caller logger for continued use. Application cleanup disposes its own logger; setup failure also releases the created child.
