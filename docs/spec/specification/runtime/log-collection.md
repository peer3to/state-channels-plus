## Purpose and observable model

A session runs across more than one thread, and each thread keeps its own logs
([execution.md](./execution.md)). The thread that hits an error is usually not the one holding the logs
that explain it. Running with less isolation means fewer threads, not fewer sets of logs.

A collection is a best-effort upload trigger across connected runtime roots. Each logger uploads its own shared store. An optional coordination service asks its other stores and connected roots to upload too. Independent top-level roots are separate unless explicitly connected. Standalone loggers can upload without a service.

## Requirements and invariants

**<a id="inv-log-1-p4wt6r"></a>`INV-LOG-1-P4WT6R` — A collection reaches connected roots.** An accepted upload trigger MUST schedule the attached stores and propagate through live root connections, including inline roots. Roots MUST suppress already handled generations. Propagation is best effort: it does not promise that every reachable root has uploaded before the caller returns.

**<a id="inv-log-2-c7kz9m"></a>`INV-LOG-2-C7KZ9M` — Collections never wait on one another.** Remote triggering MUST NOT wait for acknowledgments. A greater incoming generation is adopted before forwarding, unchanged. Equal or older generations MUST be ignored even after the coalescing window. A fresh local trigger outside that window advances the generation; nearby local triggers share it. Cycles MUST terminate without per-origin request tracking.

**<a id="req-log-1-h2vq8x"></a>`REQ-LOG-1-H2VQ8X` — Logging cleanup preserves surviving owners.** Disposing one logger MUST preserve surviving loggers sharing its resources and reconnect its children to its parent. The last logger detaches the shared store and releases its uploader without clearing buffered entries. Disposing a coordination service detaches its stores and cancels pending coordination; surviving loggers remain usable without it. An explicitly awaited local upload may finish without waiting for other roots. Logging through a disposed logger MUST fail with its component name, even when filtered. Repeated disposal remains harmless. Root disposal MUST cascade through its root logger’s descendants automatically. Application-instance disposal MUST cascade through its application logger’s descendants. Store retention MUST remain bounded by a finite configured pruning limit.

**<a id="req-log-2-n6bj3d"></a>`REQ-LOG-2-N6BJ3D` — The caller receives its local upload outcome.** An awaited logger upload MUST report whether its own attempt succeeded and how many entries it uploaded. It MUST NOT report remote success, failure counts or timeouts. A failed local attempt retains unacknowledged entries. The gossip generation records scheduled work, not successful delivery.

**<a id="req-log-3-t9fm2k"></a>`REQ-LOG-3-T9FM2K` — Writing a log line does not disturb the session.**
Writing an ordinary log line MUST NOT touch the network or disk, block protocol work, or change protocol timing
beyond a little local work. Each thread keeps only so much and throws away the oldest first. An error or explicit report may schedule asynchronous uploading. A store with
nothing new to send MUST perform no HTTP upload or jitter wait.

**<a id="req-log-4-w5xr7q"></a>`REQ-LOG-4-W5XR7Q` — Every line says where it came from.** Every log line
MUST say which session and which participant it belongs to and which thread wrote it, and MUST carry a
timestamp that can be compared against lines from the other threads. If the session or the participant is
only known later, the lines written before that MUST still end up filed under it.

**<a id="req-log-5-st6s0g"></a>`REQ-LOG-5-ST6S0G` — Sending twice does not store twice.** Sending may
happen more than once: if a send is not confirmed it MUST be sent again, and MUST NOT be treated as
stored. Sending lines that are already stored MUST NOT store them twice or put them out of order, and the
receiver MUST NOT have to remember anything between one send and the next.

**<a id="req-log-6-q8ky4n"></a>`REQ-LOG-6-Q8KY4N` — One run's logs never overwrite another's.** Logs from
different sessions, participants, threads, and runs MUST stay apart. A later run MUST NOT overwrite an
earlier one, mix into it, or push it out.

**<a id="req-log-7-m2rc5w"></a>`REQ-LOG-7-M2RC5W` — Nothing unpacks without a limit.** Neither sending nor
reading logs may require holding an unlimited amount unpacked from a limited amount sent. A receiver MUST
stop at its limit while it unpacks, rather than unpack everything and check afterwards, and MUST say when
it left something out rather than quietly return a short log.

**<a id="req-log-9-v6smac"></a>`REQ-LOG-9-V6SMAC` — An application report records its reason.** Explicit report uploading MUST record the application reason in its local logs. It MUST NOT write an aggregate reachability record without remote evidence. Remote delivery is observed at the receiver, independently of the local promise.

**<a id="req-log-10-69ctn1"></a>`REQ-LOG-10-69CTN1` — A thread that is ending waits only for its own
logs.** A thread ending because it hit an error MUST ask the threads it can reach to send theirs before it
ends, and MUST wait for its own send to finish. It MUST NOT hold its shutdown waiting for any other
thread's send.

**<a id="req-log-8-b7vn3j"></a>`REQ-LOG-8-B7VN3J` — Works wherever the runtime works.** Collection MUST
work the same whether the session runs in one thread or several, and on every supported host
([`REQ-RUNTIME-5-WJ1XKK` (Required host environments: browser and Node)](execution.md#req-runtime-5-wj1xkk)) — the same way execution itself has to match
([`INV-RUNTIME-1-AKRHAK` (Execution equivalence)](execution.md#inv-runtime-1-akrhak)). Fewer threads MAY mean fewer sets of logs;
it MUST NOT mean missing the ones that exist.

## Assumptions and constraints

- Gossip usually arrives much faster than the upload coalescing window. This is an accepted best-effort assumption, not a delivery deadline.
- Each service retains one safe integer generation. Newly connected roots synchronize that value. Invalid generations are rejected and exhausted counters must not wrap.
- Higher generations within an active window can share scheduled work. At most one local upload and one follow-up are active; entries beyond the last snapshot can wait for a later generation.

- A thread can crash, hang, or be killed with no warning, including while a collection is running.
- The thread that hits an error often has none of the logs that explain it.
- Logs are there for development and support. They are not protocol state, and no protocol decision may
  depend on them.
- The receiver may be offline, and a send can fail for one thread and work for another.
- Lines get written before the session's identity is known, so
  [`REQ-LOG-4-W5XR7Q`](log-collection.md#req-log-4-w5xr7q) cannot assume it is known when a line is
  written.
- Each thread keeps only so much ([`REQ-LOG-3-T9FM2K`](log-collection.md#req-log-3-t9fm2k)), so a stored
  log can be missing its oldest lines. Where that happened has to be visible, not silent.

## Security considerations

Logs are built out of live protocol data and out of errors, so they are a way for secrets to leak. A log
line MUST NOT carry keys, tokens, or anything else used to authorize a request, copied by accident out of
an error or out of the request that failed. When fields are copied out of an error, they MUST be picked
from a fixed list, never by copying whatever the error happens to expose.

Some of what ends up in a log is chosen by other peers, because a peer affects what a session logs about
it. A receiver MUST NOT trust a field it is sent when deciding where to put the data, MUST limit how much
one send can store, and MUST NOT let one bad send stop the rest of the session's logs from being stored.
A receiver that expects a token MUST refuse a send without one instead of storing it.

An application instance owns its logger. Setup given a caller logger creates an application child, preserving the caller logger for continued use. Application cleanup disposes its own logger; setup failure also releases the created child.
