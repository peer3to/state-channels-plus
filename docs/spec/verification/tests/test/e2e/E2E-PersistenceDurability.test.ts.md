# E2E-PersistenceDurability.test.ts

Test file: [test/e2e/E2E-PersistenceDurability.test.ts](../../../../../../../test/e2e/E2E-PersistenceDurability.test.ts)

## Overview

The persistent peers keep their channel in their own partitions. The hold cases park every batch at a peer's database (`stub.holdPersistenceFlushes`), wait until a batch reached the gate, keep it there through a 500 ms absence window and check that the signature behind it reached nobody, then release it and check that it arrives. The block case holds the next writer and one other participant: no peer holds the authored block while the author's flush is held, and once it is released no peer holds the held participant's confirmation until that flush is released. The dispute case holds the disputer, forwards its upload through the recorder and waits until the upload is recorded; no dispute commitment reaches the chain's window (read on another peer) until the release, and one does after it. The synchronization case holds both persistent participants' disks behind one re-stored block each, adds a spectator, waits until a participant's sync answer waits for its held disk (a flush waiter on its controller, `stub.getWaitingPersistenceFlushCount`), and checks the spectator is not `SYNCED` within the absence window; after the releases it syncs and follows a block. The snapshot-post case holds the poster's disk behind one re-stored block and checks the chain's snapshot is unchanged within the window, then that the post lands after the release. The promotion case holds a persistent joiner whose join landed, advances blocks until its flush after signing the block that seats it is held, and checks it is still `PENDING_PARTICIPANT` until the release, then `PARTICIPATING`. In the failure cases the next writer's database fails every batch (`stub.failPersistenceFlushes`), so the flush after it signs its block fails after its retries and the runtime aborts through the failure handler. The first failure case checks that no other peer holds a block at that height and none blacklisted the author. The second restarts the aborted author, waits for its restart synchronization to settle, lets it re-author that height from its disk, and checks that every peer agrees on the block there and no peer blacklisted it; re-authoring is safe because the unconfirmed block never left the process. The third holds every batch of a spectator's disk and checks it follows two blocks without aborting while batches wait at the gate: it signs nothing, so nothing it sends waits for its disk.
