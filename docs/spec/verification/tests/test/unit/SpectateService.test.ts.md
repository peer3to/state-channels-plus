## Overview

The declarations drive the real SpectateService through request policy, proof verification and multi-peer synchronization. Pinned requests accept a minimum height or a verified successor; delayed-install cases hold the responder's VM write and verify both peers remain unblacklisted. An unprovable height retains mutual blacklist liability. A valid proof below the requested minimum is rejected.

The policy cases distinguish initial load from pinned recovery and preserve a synced observer on caller-owned recovery failure. Snapshot races accept already-landed state. Responder cases recover committed disputes missing locally and prove the successor when local dispute events are suppressed; both wait until the window's kill period has expired before they generate a payload, because a responder serves a window only after that. Two refusal cases send a real latest request over the RPC boundary and read its error reply. In the unreadable-window case the responder's chain log queries fail, so the reply is "Dispute window unavailable", the responder does not blacklist the requester, and it builds no fraud proof. In the kill-period case a dispute is committed with a 20-second evidence period; the reply is "Kill period not expired", the requester's sync returns false while the kill period still runs, and neither peer blacklists the other. A latest request to a responder whose reduction genesis is held (its own fork behind the derived fork) is served from the derived fork without either blacklist. Four concurrency cases share the pinned-request helper (same, lower, higher height, and a shared proof failure); the reduce-data-unavailable case is a skipped placeholder, so its permutations stay unassigned. These are component scenarios through real collaborators, including the RPC boundary.

The batching case records one finality call containing two predicates and the exact rejection reason `dispute window not linked`. Its two windows are identical, so it does not test result-index mapping; that belongs to distinct-window lineage scenarios.

The linkage cases take the responder's real one-window payload for a disputed fork while the chain stays on that fork. Setting the first window's fork to its own reduced fork, or appending the same window again, is rejected with only `dispute window not linked`; the unchanged payload is accepted with no rejection. Two late-payload cases take the payload while the chain is still on the disputed fork, then let the reduction land and the chain adopt the reduced fork before applying it. Unchanged, the adopted window is skipped: the sync is accepted with no rejection and the responder stays off the blacklist. With the window's reduced fork replaced by one the chain did not record, nothing is skipped and the sync is rejected with only `dispute window not linked`. The historic rejection case pushes a forged outbound block above the on-chain snapshot and expects only `latest-fork outbound blocks invalid`.

The pinned-fork dispute case generates a payload that advances the fork, commits a dispute on that fork before the requester applies the pinned response, and asserts the historic verification accepts it with no recorded rejection and no blacklist. The historic-verification case posts a same-fork snapshot, advances two blocks, and applies a proof whose threshold is that on-chain snapshot, asserting acceptance with no rejection.
The same-fork snapshot case creates the requester before opening the four-participant channel. This keeps worker startup outside the block-zero deadline while preserving the isolated requester and the snapshot-before-validation oracle.

The verified-persistence case prepends a milestone wholly below the on-chain snapshot to the
served payload (`prependMilestoneAt`); the walk drops it unchecked, and a new spectator that syncs
from that payload stores neither its block nor its snapshot.

The `sync verification and completion` cases apply a payload on a fresh spectator whose own
initial sync is held, so it has no local history or final point (`applyOnFreshRequester` in
`test/fixtures/HistoricSyncStaging.ts`). An honest payload that advances past the on-chain
snapshot is accepted with no rejection and no blacklist, at the served height. A forged last
milestone snapshot is rejected with only `milestones invalid`, the responder is blacklisted and the
snapshot is not stored. A payload that is valid but below a newer on-chain snapshot is a stale
proof: it is rejected with only `served state below the proof start` and the responder is
blacklisted. A block-0 payload without its threshold signatures (`stageBlockZeroPayload`) is not
accepted and its block-0 snapshot is not stored.

The other completion cases run `syncOnFreshRequester` (`test/fixtures/SyncCompletionStaging.ts`) on
the same kind of fresh spectator. It accepts the empty proof of a fork with no block (head -1,
genesis state installed), a start-only proof (one run holding a posted snapshot), and that run with
unfinal blocks above it. A placeholder milestone wholly below the proof start is ignored: the sync
is accepted and neither its block nor its snapshot is stored. The tier case observes the mirror
walks: a fresh requester is answered by its local diamond with no chain walk, and a running
participant by its own final point with no storage or chain walk. A start-only proof stores the
anchor's block, snapshot and state and rebuilds a chain-valid proof from the anchor. A served unfinal
tail replays and is stored as served through the tip. When the chain snapshot moves to the served
tip before application, the served state lies below the proof start: the payload is rejected as
`served state below the proof start`, the responder is blacklisted, and nothing is stored or replayed.

The `verified historical support` cases serve separated milestones [a+1, a+2] and [a+4], where a+1
reaches its threshold only with a signature on a+2. The requester stores both blocks with their
served signers, stores no gap block and no snapshot or state for the support block, stores one
block served twice with part of its signatures each time with the union of the signers, and still
rebuilds a chain-valid proof after progress and after a later on-chain snapshot (the old blocks stay
stored). A compact proof that starts above the on-chain snapshot stores no gap block and rebuilds
from the snapshot. The explicit-retry case refuses the first chain anchor read: the apply throws
with nothing installed and no cut, and an explicit retry of the same payload is accepted.

The `verified proof persistence` cases stop the tail replay with an executor fault
(`failTailReplay`) to see what persistence stored before replay: an unfinal block-0 run stores no
block and no block-0 snapshot, and the tail above an anchor is not stored while the anchor block is.
A re-timed supplied milestone snapshot is never installed; for a run that starts at or below the
anchor the actual anchor snapshot is stored. A forged unconfirmed block below the anchor in the run,
and a run wholly below the anchor, store nothing and record no change point. Two cases repeat
behavior that is already credited and carry no ID: a re-timed last milestone snapshot is rejected as
`milestones invalid` before the honest payload installs the committed one, and a forged unsigned
milestone below the anchor is ignored like the placeholder. The repeated-run cases serve the last run
twice (support, then tail): a forged tail block is not stored from its support occurrence, a cut-off
participant replays it, rejects with `block confirmation rejected`, blacklists the responder and
disputes the fork, and the honest repetition is stored after its own replay.

The failure-consequence cases forge one element of an honest anchored payload at a time. A fresh requester applies an undecodable proof block, a forged genesis, a forged pre-genesis outbound block and a forged finalized state in one host call: each records its own rejection reason, no forged payload stores or replays a block, and the honest payload then adopts to the latest height while the requester's on-chain transaction count stays the same. A running participant applies a window on the never-disputed on-chain fork, a forged genesis, a forged pre-genesis outbound block, the honest payload pinned to another fork, and the honest payload pinned above its proved height: each rejects at its own step, the responder is blacklisted, and the participant keeps its status and head. A held answer makes a participant's exact sync fail at its round-trip bound with one strike and no blacklist. The responder refuses negative, fractional, unsafe and NaN heights before its first local snapshot read, and a fresh spectator whose own state is not installed refuses a latest request with the derived fork's missing-genesis error and does not blacklist the requester.

Two genesis cases move only the served genesis timestamp by one second, so its data, fork id and height stay genesis-shaped. A fresh requester applies a block-0 payload with the altered fork genesis before it holds any block, and a participant applies a disputed-fork payload with the altered successor genesis. Both must reject the payload and must not store the altered genesis.

The chain-final window case serves a payload for a disputed fork, lands only the reduction on chain (the chain snapshot stays on that fork) and adds a forged successor to the window's reduction-input inbound list; the requester accepts, does not store the forged block, keeps its inbound head and has nothing pending to sign. The local-reduction case applies a not chain-final window twice: a reduction that fails at the executor connection propagates its error with no rejection or cut, and a window claiming a fork its reduction does not produce is rejected and cuts the responder. The stale-queue case holds a spectator's initial sync request before its payload is built while five final blocks are queued on it; after the install, the queued blocks under the installed history are dropped, the spectator cuts no participant and keeps following.

The chain-final refusal case lands a reduction on chain only, syncs a spectator through that
window, and asks it twice for the latest state: each answer is the error reply "Reduce data unavailable
for disputed fork <forkId>" (the window's reduce input was never stored), the requester is not
blacklisted, and the spectator stays `SYNCED`.
