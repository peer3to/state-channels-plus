## Overview

These cases drive the public signer/runtime boundary. They prove input validation occurs before lifecycle mutation, the exact serializable topic and options reach the host, matching-phase leave returns true and settles the pending join, post-handoff leave returns false without cancelling negotiation, optional timeout semantics cross the port, a selected channel cannot overlap discovery, and matching chains into negotiation without a client-side internal call.
The worker direct-join case fails the join receipt through the worker API and asserts the `false` result,
restored `SYNCED` status, and a live runtime; it injects no pending fault, so the fault-interleaving
permutation stays with the end-to-end participant lifecycle suite. The lobby leave and timeout cases prove
port forwarding; the service-level leave and timeout permutations are owned by the `LobbyMatchingService`
suite, which asserts them on the service directly.
The pending-participant leave case authors through `keepAuthoringUntil` until the joiner is promoted, since the
block that carries the join is the first one whose author has already received the join's inbound event.

The ordinary lobby announcement case wraps each founder's discovery join so it records every key and still
runs the real join, opens one channel through `joinLobby`, and checks that both founders joined the opened
channel's discovery key. The closed-status case parks the leaver's exit snapshot post, lets the leaver author
its exit (absent from local state), sets its status to `NOT_OPENED` while the chain still lists it, runs the
settlement observer, and checks that the leave is unsettled with the leaver absent locally and listed on chain;
releasing the post then settles the leave and the chain no longer lists the leaver.
The evidence-expired watchdog case makes the leaver's dispute submission revert with
`RaceConditionDisputeEvidencePeriodExpired` and asserts that the leave reaches `awaiting-settlement`, one
submission was attempted, no dispute marker is recorded, and the leave promise is still unsettled. The real
expiry case parks the leaver's upload until the chain is past the window's evidence period, then forwards it:
the chain refuses it with the same error, the window reduces, the leave disputes again on the successor fork
with self-removal, and the leave settles with the leaver no longer listed.

The join-wait cases start a leave from `PENDING_PARTICIPANT` before the joiner observes its join and wait for the
`awaiting-join` phase. Their joins use a short authorization (15 to 25 seconds) so the deadline passes within the
test. When an uncertain join never lands, the founders author for one full turn window and the leave is still
in `awaiting-join`; once a chain block is past the deadline it resolves at `SYNCED` with no dispute submission.
When the join submission is held in flight and released after one turn window, before the deadline, the leave
reaches `awaiting-exit`, the runtime is not `SYNCED`, and the chain lists the joiner as pending. When the
joiner's held inbound events are replayed during the wait, the leave reaches `awaiting-exit`. When the events
stay held but the join is on chain, the wait ends after the deadline in `awaiting-exit` with the runtime still
`PENDING_PARTICIPANT`. When the wait's final chain read is held and answered from before the join landed, an
own-join observation delivered meanwhile keeps `awaiting-exit` after the late read returns, with the leave
unsettled. A final chain read that fails rejects the leave with the read error. When nobody authors, the
founders' own timeout dispute reduces the fork during the wait without settling the leave; after the deadline
it resolves at `SYNCED` and the chain does not list the joiner as pending.
